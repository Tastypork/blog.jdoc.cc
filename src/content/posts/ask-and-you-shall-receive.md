Ask and you shall receive. That is what I called this study. It is the next one in the short series on the parables Jesus told, and I think I have about fifteen of them lined up. I want us to go through them and talk about them together. We did this one on November 11, and it is two parables on prayer and persistence: the friend at midnight and the persistent widow.

<h2 style="font-size: 1.1875rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">What we discussed</h2>

We started with [Luke 11:5-8 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Luke+11:5-8/), the friend at midnight. A man goes to a friend's door in the middle of the night and asks to borrow three loaves, because another friend has shown up from a journey and he has nothing to set before him. The man inside answers, [“Do not bother me; the door is now shut, and my children are with me in bed” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Luke+11:7/). It is an analogy about neighbors. You might have asked a neighbor for some sugar. You would not show up at midnight to do it, and that is exactly the picture Jesus uses.

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">Why the neighbor gets up</h3>

I stopped at verse 8 and asked why the man gets up. He will not do it because of friendship, [“yet because of his impudence he will rise and give him whatever he needs” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Luke+11:8/). The ESV footnote says persistence. The Greek word means shamelessness, and it shows up only here in the New Testament. A simple way to read it is that the asker is bold and unembarrassed about asking.

I also read that some scholars apply the word to the man knocking, to his refusal to give up at that hour. Others say it reflects the sleeping neighbor's wish to avoid shame, because failing to help a neighbor with a guest would have brought disgrace in first-century Middle Eastern culture. Either way, the point I hung on is that there is an element of shamelessness in the asking.

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">What Jesus teaches about asking</h3>

So what is Jesus telling us about asking? When it seems extremely unlikely, persistence comes through. We should go to God boldly, unashamed, and confident, even when it feels inconvenient. That last part matters most to me. Sometimes midnight is not the clock. It is the middle of chaos, a tragedy in the family, or losing a job. How often do we go to God for what seems impossible when it is inconvenient?

If a reluctant friend will get up and help, how much more can we bring our needs to God? Someone who does not want to help is willing, and God is for us. For that I put a few verses beside the parable. [Matthew 7:7-8 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Matthew+7:7-8/): [“Ask, and it will be given to you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Matthew+7:7/). [Hebrews 4:16 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Hebrews+4:16/): [“Let us then with confidence draw near to the throne of grace” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Hebrews+4:16/). I also read [James 1:5 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/James+1:5/) and [1 Peter 5:7 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/1+Peter+5:7/) on asking God for wisdom and casting our anxieties on him.

> **Come to God boldly, even when it is inconvenient.**

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">The friend on a journey</h3>

Something I like about this parable is that the man at the door is not asking for himself. He is not saying he is hungry. He is asking for a friend who has been through a journey. I take that to mean a friend going through a hard time, someone who keeps getting beaten up. God does not only want us to come to him for ourselves, like a new phone or a new laptop. He wants us to pray inconveniently for others. That is why I pray for people at the end of a study, even when nobody asked. We are interceding for them and going out of our way to do it.

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">Coming to God without feeling unworthy</h3>

I asked whether we ever feel like we are bothering God when we pray, or feel we do not deserve to ask. In the group, a lot of us did, and the reason that came up was sin. We do not want to go to God because we are ashamed. There is something right in that. Approaching God with respect, aware that we have sinned against him, is the opposite of saying we can do whatever we want because God loves us anyway. But feeling unworthy should not keep us away. When we feel the most terrible and full of sin, that is the perfect time to go to God and talk about it with him. Church is for sinners.

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">The persistent widow</h3>

Then we read [Luke 18:1-8 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Luke+18:1-8/). Jesus tells the parable so that [“they ought always to pray and not lose heart” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Luke+18:1/). A judge who neither feared God nor respected man keeps getting visits from a widow asking for justice against her adversary. He refuses for a while, then gives in so she will not beat him down by her continual coming.

I asked what it means to pray and not lose heart, and when we are most likely to lose it. We are impatient, and one of us said that after about a month of praying with no answer, that is where it starts to slip. Someone also said to remember that God's time is best, even when you cannot see him working, and that the waiting is a trial of your faith. I liked that. I gave a few supporting verses. [1 Thessalonians 5:17 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/1+Thessalonians+5:17/) says [“pray without ceasing” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/1+Thessalonians+5:17/). [Colossians 4:2 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Colossians+4:2/) says to continue steadfastly in prayer, and [Romans 12:12 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Romans+12:12/) says to be constant in prayer.

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">How God is different from the unjust judge</h3>

Jesus is not saying God is like the judge. He is making a contrast. A hard, selfish judge finally does the right thing. How much more will God, who loves his people, hear us when we cry out to him? Jesus asks, [“will not God give justice to his elect, who cry to him day and night?” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Luke+18:7/) God is not annoyed into helping us. He is not ignoring us and hoping we will go away. He gives justice to those who call on him. For cross references I read [Psalm 68:5 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+68:5/), that God is father of the fatherless and protector of widows, and [2 Peter 3:9 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/2+Peter+3:9/), that the Lord is not slow to fulfill his promise but is patient toward us.

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">Will he find faith on earth?</h3>

The last question is in [Luke 18:8 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Luke+18:8/): [“when the Son of Man comes, will he find faith on earth?” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Luke+18:8/) Here Jesus is probing. When he returns, will he find people still trusting him enough to keep praying? That ties into what we talked about a few weeks ago on the second coming, and how we posture and prepare ourselves for the Lord's return. Will he find us trusting him, praying, and believing that he died for our sins and promised to come back and take us to everlasting life? I took the question and asked myself how I am structuring my life, and how I am using it to seek God. When he returns, who is he going to find in me? I want that to be a question I know the answer to. I want to be faithful.

> **Do it anyway. Prayer is not only for the results.**

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">Where do you need to keep praying?</h3>

I ended with an open question. Where in your life do you need to keep praying, with that same persistence, and not lose heart over what has not been delivered yet? For me it is purpose. Some days I am head on straight and moving forward. Other days I feel lost and want to lie down and give up. In those moments I need to lean on prayer and have more faith that God will use my story for good.

One question came up in the group about whether prayer is worth it if it feels pointless. I would not call that a reason to stop. Just because something feels like a waste of energy does not mean it cannot be fruitful. Prayer is not only about the result. Sometimes it builds our relationship with God, and sometimes it builds our faith in him. Do it anyway.

If you were there, you know where you landed. If you are reading this later, take the question with you before the next parable. Where do you need to keep asking?
