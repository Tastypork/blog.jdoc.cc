Where are you going to grow your bananas? That is the question I hung on this study, and it is the first one in a short series on the parables Jesus told. I want to learn them better, and I want us to talk about them. We did this one on November 3. The passage is the parable of the sower. After Matthew 13 we sat with two other pictures: a house on rock or sand, and new wine in old wineskins.

I was trying a few new things, so the video is a little extra. Bear with me.

<h2 style="font-size: 1.1875rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">What we discussed</h2>

We read Matthew 13:1-9 (ESV), then skipped to Matthew 13:18-23 (ESV), where Jesus explains the four soils. Seed on the path gets snatched. Rocky ground receives the word with joy and has no root, so when trouble comes it falls away. Thorns get choked by the cares of the world and the deceitfulness of riches. Good soil hears, understands, and bears fruit, a hundredfold, sixty, or thirty.

I asked what those soils teach about the condition of our hearts.

The path is a heart that hardens, so the word never really gets in. Rocky ground is shallow, a little soil over a hard bed of rock. Thorns are other priorities competing with the Lord. Good soil has been worked, so it can receive what is sown. Early on, somebody said that without the Lord the heart is in bad shape: pride, bitterness, nothing living in it. The soils are what that looks like when the word lands.

Here is how I had it. Jesus is showing what it looks like to receive the word with different heart postures. A hardened heart cannot receive the word of God, so the birds come and take it. In the text he calls that the evil one. I said Satan. A shallow heart may take God's words with joy and still have no deep roots, and when hard times come the faith withers like seed in the sun. A distracted heart can grow the word, but worldly worries and the deceitfulness of riches will outgrow it and stop the fruit. Only a receptive heart, one that listens, understands, and receives God's word, produces spiritual fruit.

> **Your fruit is only as good as your soil.**

I set Jeremiah 17:9-10 (ESV) and Proverbs 4:23 (ESV) beside that. Proverbs says to keep your heart with all vigilance, for from it flow the springs of life. Jeremiah says the heart is deceitful above all things and desperately sick, and that the Lord searches the heart and tests the mind, to give every person according to their ways and the fruit of their deeds. We even said the obvious thing, that the heart is what pumps the blood. That is why both verses belong here. If life flows from the heart, and the heart is also sick, then soil is not a side topic. We have to prepare the heart so the fruit of the Spirit can grow, and we have to watch it, because it wants things that are not of the Lord. Those wants become the thorns.

> **We ain't going to get our bananas that way.**

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">Why he spoke in parables</h3>

We went back for the part I had skipped, Matthew 13:10-17 (ESV). The disciples ask why he speaks in parables. He says the secrets of the kingdom of heaven have been given to them, and not to the crowds. To the one who has, more will be given. From the one who has not, even what he has will be taken away. He speaks in parables because seeing, they do not see, and hearing, they do not hear or understand.

He says the prophecy of Isaiah is fulfilled in them. That is Isaiah 6:9-10 (ESV). The sentences are not identical. In Matthew it is "you will indeed hear but never understand" and "you will indeed see but never perceive," a heart grown dull, lest they turn and he heal them. Isaiah is told to say "keep on hearing, but do not understand" and "keep on seeing, but do not perceive," and to make the heart dull, the ears heavy, and the eyes blind, lest they turn and be healed. Same passage. A dull heart can sit in front of the word and not take it in.

He does not leave the disciples there. Blessed are your eyes, for they see, and your ears, for they hear. Many prophets and righteous people longed to see what you see, and did not see it, and to hear what you hear, and did not hear it.

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">A house on the rock</h3>

Next was the two builders, Matthew 7:24-27 (ESV). Everyone who hears these words of mine and does them is like a wise man who built on the rock. The rain, the floods, and the wind beat on that house, and it stands, because it had been founded on the rock. Everyone who hears and does not do them is like a foolish man who built on the sand. Same storm. Great was the fall of it.

I asked what a strong foundation in Christ means in ordinary life, not only inside the metaphor.

A real foundation is not easily taken down. The roof can come off and you can still build, because the base does not move. Day to day, that is a faith that stays, and that keeps going back to Christ. Julia wrote it this way: maintaining strong faith by applying God's word to our life as much as we can, and not relying on our own understanding. Someone else said Christ is the piece that holds a life together. The storm might be losing a job, or something happening to the car. He is not broken by that. You can be secure in him with what you have, what you lost, and what you gain.

What I had written was the same idea, a little more specific. Center life on Christ. Ask whether his fruit is showing up, and make it right quickly when it is not. Know God's word and study it. Practice it. Do not only hear it. Pray, and stay faithful when it is hard. Jesus is not here in the body. The closest way I know to get to him is to study his word, learn the sound of his voice, understand the mission he wants us doing, the fruit of his Spirit, and build on that with each other.

The verse for that was 1 Corinthians 3:11 (ESV). No one can lay a foundation other than the one which is laid, which is Jesus Christ. In the parable, the rock is God. The rock is the Holy Spirit. That is the rock we want under the house. I also read Psalm 18:2 (ESV), because I like it. It is a very poetic verse, and it says the thing we were circling. The Lord is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer, my God, my rock in whom I take refuge, my shield and the horn of my salvation, my stronghold. He is our strength.

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">New wine and old wineskins</h3>

We closed the passages at Matthew 9:16-17 (ESV). I do not think this one is technically a parable. It still belongs. No one puts a piece of unshrunk cloth on an old garment, or the patch tears away and the tear gets worse. New wine does not go into old wineskins. If it does, the skins burst, the wine spills, and the skins are ruined. New wine goes into fresh wineskins, and both are preserved.

A wineskin that had already been used got brittle. It could not take another batch. Jesus is talking about the newness of his ministry. Old theologies and old practices are the old wineskin. Hold those, and refuse the life he is bringing, and it will not hold.

So what is that for us now? What old expectations, habits, or ideas keep us from the word God has for us?

A few things got named. Seeking approval from people, and wanting to be seen, when he has already said we are chosen, we are seen, and our identity is in him. Holding on to old sin, or keeping it hidden, and not letting God help you out of it. Skipping the ordinary work that cultivates soil: time with him, devotion, prayer, asking someone who has walked with the Lord longer than you, humility, being willing to be open. Fight those, and you hide. You also stop your own growth.

I liked that answer, and I could not place the chapter. I think it is Galatians. Sin and Christ cannot coexist as one life. Hiding your sin is a way of refusing the Lord. You cannot receive the gospel and the fruit of the Spirit, and actually follow Jesus, while you hang on to something you will not confess and will not let him help you with.

Under the question, what I had written was about the things that get trained into us until they feel normal. Bitterness picked up in a broken household. An insult from a classmate that never got put down. Substance abuse. Idols we walk past every day until we are numb to them. Calling that normal, because you grew up with it or because everyone around you is doing it, is an old wineskin. It will not hold the new wine.

Two more verses, both about being made new. Romans 12:2 (ESV): do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind, that by testing you may discern what is the will of God, what is good and acceptable and perfect. Ezekiel 36:26 (ESV): I will give you a new heart and a new spirit, remove the heart of stone, and give you a heart of flesh. Who does that renewing? God does.

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">Hearing it, or doing it?</h3>

The last question was personal. People could get specific, or keep it as vague as they wanted. Are you understanding and practicing God's word, or are you just hearing it?

I went first. There are verses I have heard a million times, and I catch myself thinking I am already done with them. The word is living. The same line can mean something now that it did not mean in grade school, when it had nothing to do with my life. The Holy Spirit can show you something new in it. If I am not diving back in, I am only hearing it.

The room was in the same place. Hearing is not doing. The stuck spot is the small things God has already asked, left undone, and then wondering why nothing is moving. What got named as a way through it was plain. Let go of pride, the sense that you have it handled when you do not. Stop hiding sin. Confess it. Give it to God. Ask for counsel. Then do the little things: wake up, read the Bible, pray, meditate on his word, look for him through the day, memorize a verse so it is there later. What they wanted was to stop living at a partial effort and put the whole heart in.

We would not let that struggle become a name. Paul says in Romans 7:15 (ESV) that he does not understand his own actions. He does not do what he wants. He does the very thing he hates. In Romans 7:18 (ESV) he says nothing good dwells in him, that is, in his flesh. He has the desire to do what is right and not the ability, in himself, to carry it out. The care in the room was the line he does not cross. Nothing good dwells in his flesh. He does not say that he is nothing good. A hard background, and a real fight with sin, is not a label the Lord puts on you, and it is not one to own. We are imperfect. We battle sin. That does not define us, because of Jesus. You can still need a Savior, and your identity can already be in him. Both. What is left is the relationship. Lean on the Lord. We talked about wanting to be close to him the way you are close to a real friend, and about letting that closeness grow on purpose. Somebody called that progressive sanctification.

If you were there, you already know which soil you were describing. If you are reading this later, sit with the question before the next parable. Are you hearing him, or are you doing what he said?

- **Matthew 13:18-23 (ESV)** — Jesus explains the soils. The path is a word the evil one snatches. Rocky ground receives the word with joy, has no root, and falls away under trouble. Thorns are choked by the cares of the world and the deceitfulness of riches. Good soil hears, understands, and bears fruit.

Isaiah is told to say: keep on hearing but do not understand, keep on seeing but do not perceive, and to make the heart dull, the ears heavy, and the eyes blind, lest the people turn and be healed.
