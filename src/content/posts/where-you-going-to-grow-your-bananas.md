Where are you going to grow your bananas? That is the question I hung on this study, and it is the first one in a short series on the parables Jesus told. I want to learn them better, and I want us to talk about them. We did this one on November 3. The passage is the parable of the sower. After Matthew 13 we sat with two other pictures: a house on rock or sand, and new wine in old wineskins.

I was trying a few new things, so the video is a little extra. Bear with me.

<h2 style="font-size: 1.1875rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">What we discussed</h2>

We read [Matthew 13:1-9 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Matthew+13:1-9/), then skipped to [Matthew 13:18-23 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Matthew+13:18-23/), where Jesus explains the four soils. Seed along the path gets snatched. Rocky ground receives the word with joy and has no root, so when trouble comes it falls away. Thorns get choked by [“the cares of the world and the deceitfulness of riches” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Matthew+13:22/). Good soil hears, understands, and bears fruit.

I asked what those soils teach about the condition of our hearts.

The path is a heart that hardens, so the word never really gets in. Rocky ground is shallow, a little soil over a hard bed of rock. Thorns are other priorities competing with the Lord. Good soil has been worked, so it can receive what is sown. Early on, somebody said that without the Lord the heart is in bad shape: pride, bitterness, nothing living in it. The soils are what that looks like when the word lands.

Here is how I had it. Jesus is showing what it looks like to receive the word with different heart postures. A hardened heart cannot receive the word of God, so the birds come and take it. In the text he calls that [“the evil one” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Matthew+13:19/). I said Satan. A shallow heart may take God's words with joy and still have no deep roots, and when hard times come the faith withers like seed in the sun. A distracted heart can grow the word, but those cares and riches will outgrow it and stop the fruit. Only a receptive heart, one that listens, understands, and receives God's word, produces spiritual fruit.

> **Your fruit is only as good as your soil.**

I set [Jeremiah 17:9-10 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Jeremiah+17:9-10/) and [Proverbs 4:23 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Proverbs+4:23/) beside that. Proverbs says to [“keep your heart with all vigilance” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Proverbs+4:23/), because life flows from it. Jeremiah says the heart is [“deceitful above all things, and desperately sick” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Jeremiah+17:9/), and that the Lord searches it. We even said the obvious thing, that the heart is what pumps the blood. That is why both verses belong here. If life flows from the heart, and the heart is also sick, then soil is not a side topic. We have to prepare the heart so the fruit of the Spirit can grow, and we have to watch it, because it wants things that are not of the Lord. Those wants become the thorns.

> **We ain't going to get our bananas that way.**

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">Why he spoke in parables</h3>

We went back for the part I had skipped, [Matthew 13:10-17 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Matthew+13:10-17/). The disciples ask why he speaks in parables. He says the secrets of the kingdom have been given to them, not to the crowds, and that what a person already has is either increased or taken away. He speaks in parables because some people see and still do not see, and hear and still do not understand.

He says the prophecy of Isaiah is fulfilled in them. That is [Isaiah 6:9-10 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Isaiah+6:9-10/). The sentences are not identical. Matthew has [“you will indeed hear but never understand” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Matthew+13:14/) and [“you will indeed see but never perceive” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Matthew+13:14/). Isaiah is told, [“Keep on hearing, but do not understand” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Isaiah+6:9/) and [“keep on seeing, but do not perceive” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Isaiah+6:9/). Same passage. A dull heart can sit in front of the word and not take it in.

He does not leave the disciples there. Blessed are the eyes that see and the ears that hear. Many prophets and righteous people longed to see what the disciples were seeing, and did not.

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">A house on the rock</h3>

Next was the two builders, [Matthew 7:24-27 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Matthew+7:24-27/). Hear his words and do them, and the house is on the rock. The storm hits and it stands. Hear and do not do them, and the house is on the sand. Same storm, and [“great was the fall of it” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Matthew+7:27/).

I asked what a strong foundation in Christ means in ordinary life, not only inside the metaphor.

A real foundation is not easily taken down. The roof can come off and you can still build, because the base does not move. Day to day, that is a faith that stays, and that keeps going back to Christ. Julia wrote it this way: maintaining strong faith by applying God's word to our life as much as we can, and not relying on our own understanding. Someone else said Christ is the piece that holds a life together. The storm might be losing a job, or something happening to the car. He is not broken by that. You can be secure in him with what you have, what you lost, and what you gain.

What I had written was the same idea, a little more specific. Center life on Christ. Ask whether his fruit is showing up, and make it right quickly when it is not. Know God's word and study it. Practice it. Do not only hear it. Pray, and stay faithful when it is hard. Jesus is not here in the body. The closest way I know to get to him is to study his word, learn the sound of his voice, understand the mission he wants us doing, the fruit of his Spirit, and build on that with each other.

The verse for that was [1 Corinthians 3:11 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/1+Corinthians+3:11/): [“no one can lay a foundation other than that which is laid, which is Jesus Christ” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/1+Corinthians+3:11/). In the parable, the rock is God. The rock is the Holy Spirit. That is the rock we want under the house. I also read [Psalm 18:2 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+18:2/), because I like it. It is a very poetic verse. [“The LORD is my rock and my fortress and my deliverer” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Psalm+18:2/). He is our strength.

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">New wine and old wineskins</h3>

We closed the passages at [Matthew 9:16-17 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Matthew+9:16-17/). I do not think this one is technically a parable. It still belongs. [“No one puts a piece of unshrunk cloth on an old garment” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Matthew+9:16/), or the patch tears a worse hole. New wine does not belong in old wineskins. [“New wine is put into fresh wineskins” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Matthew+9:17/), and both are kept.

A wineskin that had already been used got brittle. It could not take another batch. Jesus is talking about the newness of his ministry. Old theologies and old practices are the old wineskin. Hold those, and refuse the life he is bringing, and it will not hold.

So what is that for us now? What old expectations, habits, or ideas keep us from the word God has for us?

A few things got named. Seeking approval from people, and wanting to be seen, when he has already said we are chosen, we are seen, and our identity is in him. Holding on to old sin, or keeping it hidden, and not letting God help you out of it. Skipping the ordinary work that cultivates soil: time with him, devotion, prayer, asking someone who has walked with the Lord longer than you, humility, being willing to be open. Fight those, and you hide. You also stop your own growth.

I liked that answer, and I could not place the chapter. I think it is Galatians. Sin and Christ cannot coexist as one life. Hiding your sin is a way of refusing the Lord. You cannot receive the gospel and the fruit of the Spirit, and actually follow Jesus, while you hang on to something you will not confess and will not let him help you with.

Under the question, what I had written was about the things that get trained into us until they feel normal. Bitterness picked up in a broken household. An insult from a classmate that never got put down. Substance abuse. Idols we walk past every day until we are numb to them. Calling that normal, because you grew up with it or because everyone around you is doing it, is an old wineskin. It will not hold the new wine.

Two more verses, both about being made new. [Romans 12:2 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Romans+12:2/): [“Do not be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewal of your mind” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Romans+12:2/). [Ezekiel 36:26 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Ezekiel+36:26/) says [“I will give you a new heart” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Ezekiel+36:26/) and take out the heart of stone. Who does that renewing? God does.

<h3 style="font-size: 1.05rem; font-weight: 600; line-height: 1.35;">Hearing it, or doing it?</h3>

The last question was personal. People could get specific, or keep it as vague as they wanted. Are you understanding and practicing God's word, or are you just hearing it?

I went first. There are verses I have heard a million times, and I catch myself thinking I am already done with them. The word is living. The same line can mean something now that it did not mean in grade school, when it had nothing to do with my life. The Holy Spirit can show you something new in it. If I am not diving back in, I am only hearing it.

The room was in the same place. Hearing is not doing. The stuck spot is the small things God has already asked, left undone, and then wondering why nothing is moving. What got named as a way through it was plain. Let go of pride, the sense that you have it handled when you do not. Stop hiding sin. Confess it. Give it to God. Ask for counsel. Then do the little things: wake up, read the Bible, pray, meditate on his word, look for him through the day, memorize a verse so it is there later. What they wanted was to stop living at a partial effort and put the whole heart in.

We would not let that struggle become a name. Paul says in [Romans 7:15 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Romans+7:15/), [“I do not understand my own actions” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Romans+7:15/). In [Romans 7:18 (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Romans+7:18/) he says [“nothing good dwells in me, that is, in my flesh” (ESV)](https://www.esv.org/Romans+7:18/). The care in the room was the line he does not cross. The struggle sits in the flesh. He does not say that he himself is nothing good. A hard background, and a real fight with sin, is not a label the Lord puts on you, and it is not one to own. We are imperfect. We battle sin. That does not define us, because of Jesus. You can still need a Savior, and your identity can already be in him. Both. What is left is the relationship. Lean on the Lord. We talked about wanting to be close to him the way you are close to a real friend, and about letting that closeness grow on purpose. Somebody called that progressive sanctification.

If you were there, you already know which soil you were describing. If you are reading this later, sit with the question before the next parable. Are you hearing him, or are you doing what he said?

- **Matthew 13:10-17 (NASB)** — 10 And the disciples came up and said to Him, “Why do You speak to them in parables?” 11 And Jesus answered them, “To you it has been granted to know the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven, but to them it has not been granted. 12 For whoever has, to him more shall be given, and he will have an abundance; but whoever does not have, even what he has shall be taken away from him. 13 Therefore I speak to them in parables; because while seeing they do not see, and while hearing they do not hear, nor do they understand. 14 And in their case the prophecy of Isaiah is being fulfilled, which says, ‘You shall keep on listening, but shall not understand; And you shall keep on looking, but shall not perceive; 15 For the heart of this people has become dull, With their ears they scarcely hear, And they have closed their eyes, Otherwise they might see with their eyes, Hear with their ears, Understand with their heart, and return, And I would heal them.’ 16 But blessed are your eyes, because they see; and your ears, because they hear. 17 For truly I say to you that many prophets and righteous people longed to see what you see, and did not see it, and to hear what you hear, and did not hear it.”
- **Matthew 13:18-23 (NASB)** — 18 “Listen then to the parable of the sower. 19 When anyone hears the word of the kingdom and does not understand it, the evil one comes and snatches away what has been sown in his heart. This is the one sown with seed beside the road. 20 The one sown with seed on the rocky places, this is the one who hears the word and immediately receives it with joy; 21 yet he has no firm root in himself, but is only temporary, and when affliction or persecution occurs because of the word, immediately he falls away. 22 And the one sown with seed among the thorns, this is the one who hears the word, and the anxiety of the world and the deceitfulness of wealth choke the word, and it becomes unfruitful. 23 But the one sown with seed on the good soil, this is the one who hears the word and understands it, who indeed bears fruit and produces, some a hundred, some sixty, and some thirty times as much.”

- **Jeremiah 17:9-10 (NASB)** — 9 “The heart is more deceitful than all else And is desperately sick; Who can understand it? 10 I, the Lord, search the heart, I test the mind, To give to each person according to his ways, According to the results of his deeds.”
- **Proverbs 4:23 (NASB)** — 23 Watch over your heart with all diligence, For from it flow the springs of life.
- **Matthew 7:24-27 (NASB)** — 24 “Therefore, everyone who hears these words of Mine, and acts on them, will be like a wise man who built his house on the rock. 25 And the rain fell and the floods came, and the winds blew and slammed against that house; and yet it did not fall, for it had been founded on the rock. 26 And everyone who hears these words of Mine, and does not act on them, will be like a foolish man who built his house on the sand. 27 And the rain fell and the floods came, and the winds blew and slammed against that house; and it fell—and its collapse was great.”
- **Psalm 18:2 (NASB)** — 2 The Lord is my rock and my fortress and my savior, My God, my rock, in whom I take refuge; My shield and the horn of my salvation, my stronghold.
